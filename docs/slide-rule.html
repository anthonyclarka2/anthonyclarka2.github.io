Working

    Why this instrument is interesting

    A slide rule has no gears, no digits and no memory. It is two sticks of wood with numbers printed on them, and it multiplies because the distance from 1 to 2 is the same on every logarithmic scale, so setting one stick's 1 against a number and reading another number against the stock adds their logarithms — which is what multiplication is.

    That single idea buys multiplication, division, squares, roots, cubes, common logarithms, sines and tangents. It buys none of addition, because there is no scale that adds a distance to another.

    Sines are read from the CI scale rather than D, which surprises most people: under a graduation on S, the D scale shows the cosecant, not the sine. There is no cosine scale either — cos 60° is read as sin 30°, the sine of the complement.

    The four decade scale is short. Operands more than four decades from the index have to be shifted, and the narration says when that happens. Tangent above 45° falls off the left of the index onto the part of the slide that overhangs the stock, exactly as on a physical rule.
